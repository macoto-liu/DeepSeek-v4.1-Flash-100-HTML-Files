From the editor

A short defence
of the off-cut

Every good print shop keeps a drawer of mistakes. Not the ruined ones — those go in the bin with ceremony — but the almosts. The sheet where the blue arrived a breath early. The cover whose magenta wandered three millimetres south, as if looking for something it had dropped.

We print this issue in two passes, on a machine that groans like a small animal. The drum is older than most of the staff. When it is cold, it lies; when it is warm, it lies differently. We have stopped calling this error and started calling it weather.

“The accident is
the only part
nobody else can print.”

So: leave the misregistration where you find it. Pull the proof, pin it up, and look at the little coloured shadow that proves a human being was standing right there, deciding when to stop.

— M. Okonjo, editor
Leeds, 14 October
