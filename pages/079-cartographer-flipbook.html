Every evening he mapped the same harbour. Every morning the harbour had moved.

This is the story of what he found between the last line and the first — a window, a small boat, and a coast that refused to be finished.

The Cartographer's SleepI
Chapter One · The Map

The Map That Remembered

He drew the coast from memory, and the coast obliged.

There were streets he had drawn so often that they had begun to look back. There were stairs that ended in the sea, and a sea that ended in a sentence he had not yet written.

At the bottom of the sheet, the ink gathered into a small boat. He decided not to erase it.

The Cartographer's SleepII
Interlude

The Window Facing West

At the hour when the lamps are lit but the sky has not decided, he let the evening in.

It came in with salt on its hands and sat in the good chair. The map on the desk lifted at one corner, as if listening.

He wrote: the west is a rumour the evening keeps telling. Then he crossed it out, more neatly.

Chapter Two · The Sleeper

The Sleeping Cartographer

Somewhere between the third cape and the fourth, he put down his pen.

The map continued without him. A coastline unspooled beneath his folded arms; a new island arrived quietly in the margin, as if it had been waiting for the room to grow quiet.

He dreamed of a sea that could be folded and put in a pocket. In the dream, it was still wet.

Fig. 3 — The map, continuing without permission

The Cartographer's SleepIV
Interlude

A Boat, Unmoored

That night a small boat came loose from the drawing and sailed the length of the desk.

It carried no one, and it carried everyone. The compass turned to watch it go. The candle leaned after it, then thought better of the idea.

By morning there would be a tide line on the desk, and a story he would have to draw smaller to keep.

The Cartographer's SleepV
Chapter Three · Morning

Morning, and the Sea

He woke to gulls, or to the sound of a pen being drawn across paper.

The harbour was exactly where he had left it, which was the first surprise. The second was the boat: drawn in the margin, dry, patient, waiting for a tide.

He made tea. He opened the window. He began, very carefully, to draw the morning.
