There is a particular black that does not announce itself. It sits at the edge of the light, takes the shape of the room, and returns only a narrow gold seam where the hand has been.

Obsidian is cut from a single block, then faceted on four planes before nine stages of polishing. The result is not a mirror. It is something quieter: a surface that keeps its thoughts to itself.

Each piece is finished in Antwerp and numbered beneath the base. No two seams fall in the same place. The weight is deliberate — heavy enough to stay where it is put, light enough to be moved when the light changes.

“The object does not fill the room. It gives the room a centre.”
